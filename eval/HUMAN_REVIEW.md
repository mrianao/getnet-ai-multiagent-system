# Human review rubric (calibration of the automatic metrics)

Automatic metrics (fact recall, forbidden claims, citation validity, the groundedness proxy) are
cheap and reproducible, but they are not ground truth. A reviewer labels every answer of a run in
`eval/results/<label>/human_review.csv`, then the agreement between human labels and automatic
verdicts is reported in `docs/evaluation.md`.

Open the official source URLs listed in the `sources` column when judging.

| Score | Correctness | Groundedness | Usefulness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | Everything stated is correct for Getnet / the merchant's (simulated) data | Every factual statement is supported by the cited official page or tool data | Directly answers the question with actionable information |
| **1** | Mostly correct; a minor inaccuracy or imprecision | Mostly supported; at most one unsupported but harmless detail | Partially answers, or needs a follow-up |
| **0** | Contains a wrong fact that could mislead the merchant | Contains an unsupported procedure, number or claim (e.g. card-activation steps in an antecipação answer) | Does not answer / irrelevant |

Rules:
- Judge the answer as a merchant would receive it; ignore formatting.
- A refusal / "not found" that is correct for the corpus scores Correctness 2, Groundedness 2, Usefulness ≤ 1.
- Simulated customer data counts as ground truth for Customer Support answers.
- Write the reason for any 0 in `notes`.

Status: the sheets are generated by `eval/run_eval.py`; **labels are pending** (to be filled by a
human reviewer — they are intentionally not produced by the model under evaluation).
